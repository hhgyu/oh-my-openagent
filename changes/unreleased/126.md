## 2026-11-27 - Coalesce same-process memory receipt appends (#126)

Each `appendMemoryReceipt` / `appendMemoryReceiptOnce` call took the cross-process `receipts.lock` separately, even inside one process, so N concurrent appends became N pollers that noticed a release only at their next poll (up to 250 ms), each paying a lock-publish fsync and an append fsync. The Windows CI race test (20 local appends plus 3 child processes of 10) spent seconds idle and once ran past its 30 s budget. A per-runtime-dir in-memory queue now drains every queued append under one lock acquisition with one `appendFile(flush: true)`. Callers resolve after that write returns, batches and lines stay in submission order, and a once-only append is checked against the file plus the lines queued ahead of it. Cross-process exclusivity, the lock timeout and crash behavior (append-only, a trailing partial line is skipped on read) are unchanged. Measured on the race test, the median went from 650 ms to 153 ms on Windows and from 642 ms to 81 ms on WSL.
