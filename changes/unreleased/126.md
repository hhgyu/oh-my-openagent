## 2026-11-27 - Coalesce same-process memory receipt appends (#126)

Each `appendMemoryReceipt` / `appendMemoryReceiptOnce` call took the cross-process `receipts.lock` separately, even inside one process, so N concurrent appends became N pollers that noticed a release only at their next poll (up to 250 ms), each paying a lock-publish fsync and an append fsync. The Windows CI race test (20 local appends plus 3 child processes of 10) spent seconds idle and once ran past its 30 s budget. A per-runtime-dir in-memory queue now drains every queued append under one lock acquisition with one `appendFile(flush: true)`. Callers resolve after that write returns, batches and lines stay in submission order, and a once-only append is checked against the file plus the lines queued ahead of it. Each queued caller keeps its own 30 s deadline from the moment it submits: if its batch has not been admitted under the lock by then it rejects with `LockContentionError` and is removed from the batch, so a rejected line is never written later. The deadline is re-checked against the clock when a batch is admitted, so a late timer cannot let an overdue line through. At most 4096 appends wait per runtime dir; one more is rejected at once with the same error and never written. A flush takes at most 256 appends (the rest wait for the next flush under their own deadlines) and waits for the lock no longer than the latest queued deadline. Cross-process exclusivity and crash behavior (append-only, a trailing partial line is skipped on read) are unchanged. Measured on the race test, the median went from 650 ms to 86 ms on Windows and from 642 ms to 91 ms on WSL.
